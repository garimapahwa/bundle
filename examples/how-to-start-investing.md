## Script ideas

### 1. I Invested $100 For 30 Days. Here's the Exact Account, Fees, and First Trade I Made

**Hook:** Most beginner investing videos tell you what to do. I'm going to show you what I actually did with a hundred dollars and the three mistakes I almost made in the first week.

- Open on the real account screen: the broker, the account type, and the $100 deposit, with no reassurance first.
- Walk through the first purchase: how you choose an index fund or ETF, and how to read its expense ratio on screen.
- Show the one fee that surprised you (account, transfer, or spread) and how to check for it before funding any account.
- Show day 30 numbers honestly, including the dip, and explain why you did not sell.
- End with a 3-step checklist a viewer can finish this weekend.

### 2. The Investing Mistake That Costs Beginners More Than Bad Stock Picks

**Hook:** A lot of new investors worry about picking the wrong stock. The mistake that actually costs them the most has nothing to do with picking. It's the one a broker app nudges you toward on day one.

- Define the mistake: trading too often, or buying in a leveraged or options product by default.
- Use a simple calculation to show how a 1% yearly fee or frequent trading compounds into a large gap over 30 years.
- Show how to find the setting or product menu where the default is set in a common broker app.
- Give the fix: switch the default, set one automatic monthly contribution, and stop checking the balance daily.
- Close with what to do in the first 10 minutes of setup.

### 3. Starting to Invest at 35 With a Job and Kids: What I Did Differently From the Teen Videos

**Hook:** Every video telling you to start investing at 13 is useless if you're 35, have a mortgage, and two kids. So here is the starting plan I used when I was behind.

- State the situation clearly: age, income range, debts, and how much monthly cash flow is realistic.
- Explain the order of steps: emergency fund size, paying off high-interest debt, then retirement accounts with employer matching.
- Show the actual monthly budget line for investing, using a real or realistic example.
- Address the catch-up question: how contribution increases each year change the outcome, using simple numbers.
- Finish with a one-page starting sequence viewers can screenshot and follow.
